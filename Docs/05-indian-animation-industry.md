# 05 — The Indian Animation and VFX Industry

## 1. What is "AVGC-XR"?

In India, the sector is officially called **AVGC-XR**:

| Letter | Stands for |
|---|---|
| **A** | Animation |
| **V** | Visual Effects (VFX) |
| **G** | Gaming |
| **C** | Comics |
| **XR** | Extended Reality (AR + VR + MR) |

The term is used by the **Ministry of Information & Broadcasting (MIB)**, the **AVGC Promotion Task Force (2022)**, IICT and state policies, including Karnataka's.

> **Note on conflicting numbers:** Different research firms define "animation" differently (some include VFX and gaming, some don't), so figures range from **~US$300 million to over US$2 billion**. For the website, use the FICCI-EY figures and always name the source.

### Revenue structure (how money flows)
1. **Service / outsourcing work** for Hollywood and global studios: the largest share, especially VFX (roto, paint, matchmove, compositing, animation, lighting)
2. **Domestic VFX** for Indian films (Telugu, Tamil, Hindi and Kannada blockbusters)
3. **Original IP**: kids' TV (*Chhota Bheem*, *Motu Patlu*, *Little Singham*), features (*Mahavatar Narsimha*), OTT series (*The Legend of Hanuman*)
4. **Advertising**: 3D commercials, product visualisation
5. **Gaming**: art outsourcing plus original mobile games
6. **Licensing and merchandise**: toys, apparel, books (big for *Chhota Bheem*)

## 6. The 2025 shock: Technicolor's collapse

- In **February 2025**, the France-based **Technicolor Group** went into liquidation. Its Indian operations (**MPC, Mikros Animation, Technicolor Games, The Mill**), with offices in **Bengaluru** and Mumbai, shut down suddenly.
- About **2,000–3,200 employees** were affected (reports vary), many with unpaid salaries.
- **Lessons for students:** service-work dependency is a risk. Diversify skills, build a strong portfolio, keep savings, and consider original Indian IP, gaming and tech roles.
- Much of the talent moved to DNEG, Assemblage, Framestore, gaming studios and freelance work.

## 7. SWOT analysis: Indian 3D animation

| **Strengths** | **Weaknesses** |
|---|---|
| Large, skilled, English-speaking talent pool | Over-dependence on outsourced service work |
| Cost-competitive world-class quality | Low pay at entry level; long hours |
| Strong IT and engineering backbone (esp. Bengaluru) | Few original Indian IPs at global scale |
| Huge mythological and folklore story library | Training quality varies; many low-quality institutes |
| Multi-language domestic market | Limited funding for original animated features |

| **Opportunities** | **Threats** |
|---|---|
| *Mahavatar Narsimha* proved Indian animated films can earn big | Generative AI automating junior tasks (roto, cleanup, in-betweening) |
| OTT platforms need local kids' and YA content | Global studio closures (Technicolor 2025) |
| Government support: IICT, WAVES, state policies | Competition from other countries (Canada, UK, Korea, Philippines) |
| Gaming boom; mobile-first audience | Piracy; tax/incentive uncertainty |
| Regional-language animation (Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Bengali) | Talent drain abroad |
| Virtual production, XR, real-time engines | — |
