# 11 — Learning Roadmap: From Zero to Job-Ready

> Designed for an Indian student with a laptop/PC, limited budget and possibly no formal art training. Uses **free tools first**.

---

## Stage 0: Before you start (Week 0)
- [ ] Check your PC meets the minimum spec (see [04-software-and-hardware.md](04-software-and-hardware.md)).
- [ ] Install **Blender** (free, from blender.org only, never from third-party sites).
- [ ] Get a sketchbook and pencil. Drawing daily helps even 3D artists.
- [ ] Create accounts on **ArtStation**, **YouTube** and **LinkedIn**.
- [ ] Set a schedule: **1–2 hours a day, 5–6 days a week**.

## Stage 1: Foundations (Months 1–2)
**Goal:** understand the 3D interface and basic modelling.
- Blender interface, navigation, shortcuts
- Basic modelling: a cup, table, chair, simple room
- Materials and basic lighting
- Render your first image
- **Classic beginner project:** Blender Guru's "Donut" tutorial (YouTube, free)
- **Indian-themed mini projects:** a **steel tumbler and dabba**, a **chai cup and kettle**, a **Channapatna toy**, a **diya**, an **auto-rickshaw**

**Art fundamentals in parallel:** perspective, basic shapes, light and shadow, colour theory.

## Stage 2: Core skills (Months 3–5)
- **Modelling:** hard-surface (a scooter, a phone) and organic (simple character)
- **UV unwrapping and texturing** (Blender, then Substance 3D Painter if available)
- **Lighting:** three-point lighting, HDRI, mood
- **Animation basics:** the **12 principles** (see [03-production-pipeline.md](03-production-pipeline.md))
  - Bouncing ball → ball with tail → pendulum → flour sack → walk cycle
- **Camera and composition**

## Stage 3: Choose a specialisation (Months 6–9)

| If you love… | Specialise in | Learn |
|---|---|---|
| Making characters move and act | **Character Animation** | Body mechanics, acting, lip-sync. Maya (edu licence) + Blender |
| Building things | **Modelling / Sculpting** | ZBrush/Blender Sculpt, anatomy, topology |
| Painting and materials | **Texturing / Look-dev** | Substance, shaders, renderers |
| Physics and effects | **FX** | **Houdini Apprentice** (free), VEX |
| Puzzles and code | **Rigging / Technical** | Maya rigging, **Python** |
| Mood and photography | **Lighting** | Lighting theory, Arnold/Cycles/Karma |
| Putting it all together | **Compositing** | Nuke Non-commercial, DaVinci Fusion |
| Games | **Game Art / Tech Art** | Low/high-poly, baking, **Unreal Engine 5** |
| Architecture and interiors | **ArchViz** | 3ds Max + Corona/V-Ray, or Blender + D5/Twinmotion |
| Brands and ads | **Motion Graphics / Product Viz** | Cinema 4D / Blender + After Effects |

## Stage 4: Portfolio and demo reel (Months 9–12)
- Create **3–5 strong, finished pieces** in your specialisation.
- Make a **demo reel of 60–90 seconds**: **best work first**, no long intros, music optional.
- Include **breakdowns** (wireframe, clay, texture passes) to show your process.
- Put your name, role and contact on the first and last frames.
- Upload to **ArtStation + Vimeo/YouTube**, and link it on **LinkedIn**.
- Tailor your reel to the job (animation reel ≠ modelling reel).

### Demo reel do's and don'ts
| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Show only your best work | Include everything you ever made |
| Credit what you did in team projects | Claim others' work |
| Show original work and studies | Use only tutorial copies (e.g., only the donut) |
| Keep it short and punchy | Make it 5 minutes long |
| Put Indian themes in if you like; they stand out | Use copyrighted characters as your main showcase |

## Stage 5: Get hired / freelance (Month 12+)
- Apply to studios (DNEG, Xentrix, game studios, ad agencies, ArchViz firms) and keep an eye on **LinkedIn, Naukri, ArtStation Jobs, studio career pages**.
- Attend **Bengaluru GAFX**, college fests and meetups (Blender India communities, Unreal meetups).
- Do **internships**. Even short ones help.
- Start freelancing: **Fiverr, Upwork, Behance, Instagram**, or local businesses (real-estate walkthroughs, product renders for D2C brands).
- Take part in challenges: **Blender community challenges, 11 Second Club (animation), ArtStation challenges, CGTrader/Polycount contests**.

## Free learning resources

### Official and community (free)
| Resource | What |
|---|---|
| **Blender Manual and Blender Studio** (studio.blender.org) | Official docs; Blender Studio has paid premium courses plus free content |
| **Unreal Engine Online Learning / Epic Developer Community** | Free official Unreal courses |
| **SideFX Learn** | Free Houdini tutorials |
| **Autodesk Maya Learning Channel** | Free official Maya tutorials |
| **Foundry Learn** | Nuke tutorials |
| **NPTEL / SWAYAM** | Indian government free online courses (design, computer graphics) |

### YouTube channels (English)
- **Blender Guru** (Andrew Price), **Grant Abbitt**, **CG Cookie**, **Ryan King Art**, **Default Cube**, **Ian Hubert** (quick tips), **Josh Gambrell** (hard surface)
- **Alan Becker** (animation principles), **Sir Wade Neistadt** (animation), **Morgan Williams**
- **Unreal Sensei**, **William Faucher** (Unreal cinematics)
- **Entagma** (Houdini), **Nine Between**

### Indian / Hindi-language creators
- There is a large and growing Hindi and regional-language Blender/Maya tutorial community on YouTube. ⚠️ The website team should curate and verify a current list of active Indian creators (including any **Kannada-language** 3D tutorial channels) before publishing, and refresh it periodically.

### Books
- *The Animator's Survival Kit*, Richard Williams
- *The Illusion of Life: Disney Animation*, Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston
- *Digital Lighting and Rendering*, Jeremy Birn
- *Color and Light*, James Gurney
- *Figure Drawing for All It's Worth*, Andrew Loomis
- *The Art of Pixar* series (inspiration)

### Paid but highly regarded online schools
- **CG Spectrum**, **Animation Mentor**, **iAnimate**, **AnimSchool** (character animation)
- **Gnomon Workshop**, **CGMA**, **Rebelway** (FX/Houdini), **Think Tank Training Centre**
- **Udemy / Coursera / Domestika**: affordable courses, often heavily discounted in India

## A sample weekly plan (for a college student)

| Day | Activity (1.5–2 hrs) |
|---|---|
| Mon | Tutorial: new technique |
| Tue | Practise the technique on your own object |
| Wed | Drawing / fundamentals (perspective, anatomy, gesture) |
| Thu | Personal project work |
| Fri | Personal project work |
| Sat | Watch film breakdowns / analyse an animated scene frame by frame; post progress online |
| Sun | Rest, or join a community challenge |
